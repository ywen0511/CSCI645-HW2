\section*{Summary of what was run}
A PPO teacher was trained on \texttt{PandaPickCube}, evaluated on
\nEvalPerSeed{} held-out initial conditions, and used to collect three
demonstration pools (clean, and with Gaussian action noise
$\sigma\!\in\!\{0.1,0.3\}$ injected \emph{during} environment interaction).
ACT students were then trained on nested subsets of the clean pool
(25/50/100 demonstrations) and on the two noisy pools, \nSeeds{} random seeds
each, and every student was evaluated on the \emph{same} \nEvalPerSeed{}
held-out initial conditions. A flow-matching policy was trained on the clean
100-demonstration pool as an additional modern baseline. Table~\ref{tab:main}
is the headline result; Table~\ref{tab:paired} gives paired statistics.

\section{Task and demonstration source}
\textbf{Environment.} \texttt{PandaPickCube} from MuJoCo Playground: a Franka
Emika Panda must bring a box to a target pose sampled uniformly in a
$0.4\,\mathrm{m}\times0.4\,\mathrm{m}$ box in $x,y$ and $0.2$--$0.4\,\mathrm{m}$
\emph{above} the table, so the task requires reaching, grasping and lifting.
Observations are the \obsDim-dimensional state vector built by the
environment's \texttt{\_get\_obs} (joint positions and velocities, gripper
position and orientation, box orientation, box-relative and target-relative
offsets, and control residuals). Actions are \actDim{}-dimensional joint-space
deltas in $[-1,1]$, scaled by \texttt{action\_scale}$=0.04$ and added to the
current control, which is then clipped to the actuator limits. Episodes are
\epLen{} control steps at $\ctrlDt\,\mathrm{s}$ (3\,s of simulated time).

\textbf{Defining success.} This is a necessary design decision rather than a
detail: the environment exposes \emph{no} success signal. Its \texttt{done}
flag fires only when the box leaves the workspace or the simulation produces
NaNs, so episodes otherwise always run the full \epLen{} steps, and the only
task-progress field in \texttt{state.info} is \texttt{reached\_box} (gripper
within $12\,\mathrm{mm}$ of the box). We therefore define success
geometrically:
\begin{quote}
an episode succeeds if the box comes within \successRadiusCm{}\,cm of the
target and \emph{stays} there for \successHold{} consecutive control steps
($\successHoldSec\,$s).
\end{quote}
The hold requirement is what stops a fast fly-through of the target region from
counting as a pick-and-place. Because the reported numbers depend on this
threshold, we also report success as a function of the radius so the reader can
see how much of the result is threshold-sensitivity (Section~\ref{sec:fail}).

\textbf{Teacher.} PPO from Brax, using the hyperparameters that MuJoCo
Playground ships for this task (\texttt{manipulation\_params.brax\_ppo\_config}):
\teacherNumEnvs{} parallel environments, learning rate $10^{-3}$, discount
$0.97$, entropy cost $0.02$, observation normalisation on, policy MLP
$(32,32,32,32)$ and value MLP $(256,256,256,256,256)$. \emph{Deviation:} we
trained for \teacherSteps{} environment steps instead of the shipped
$20{,}000{,}000$, because at 20\,M the policy had not yet become a useful
demonstrator. Figure~\ref{fig:ppo} shows the learning curve, from which the
reader can see what 20\,M would have given. Training took
\teacherWallMin{} minutes on one \gpuName{}. Demonstrations are recorded from
the \emph{deterministic} (mean) action of the final policy.

\section{Demonstration quality}
The clean pool contains \datasigmaZeroEps{} episodes
(\datasigmaZeroTrans{} transitions), with mean length
$\datasigmaZeroLenMean \pm \datasigmaZeroLenStd$ control steps. Lengths are
almost constant because the environment does not terminate on success --- every
episode runs to the horizon --- so length carries little information here, which
is itself worth noting when interpreting "trajectory length" statistics for this
benchmark.

action distribution shows which joints the teacher actually drives.

\textbf{Consistency, coverage and visible inefficiencies.} The teacher is a
single deterministic policy, so trajectories starting from nearby initial
conditions are highly consistent --- there is no multi-modality of the kind that
motivates generative policies, which matters when interpreting the ACT-versus-
flow-matching comparison later. The inefficiencies that \emph{are} visible are
(i) the policy often approaches the box along a curved path rather than
directly, and (ii) once near the target it dithers instead of settling, which is
what the hold requirement in our success definition penalises.

\section{Policy and training}
We chose \textbf{ACT} (Action Chunking with Transformers). Inputs are the
normalised \obsDim-dimensional state; outputs are a chunk of $H=\chunkSize$
future actions (\actDim{} dimensions each), i.e. $\chunkSize\times\ctrlDt$
seconds of open-loop action. The model is the transformer CVAE of the ACT paper:
a posterior encoder sees the ground-truth action chunk and the observation and
emits a style latent $z$; a transformer encoder-decoder with $H$ learned
decoder queries maps $(z,\text{obs})$ to the chunk; the loss is an $L_1$
reconstruction term on the chunk plus $\beta\,\mathrm{KL}$ with $\beta=10$. At
test time $z$ is set to the prior mean. Padded chunk steps at the end of an
episode are excluded from the loss by a mask.

\textbf{Deviations from the reference implementation}, all deliberate: no
ResNet image backbone (observations are state, so the conditioning token is a
linear projection of the state vector); pre-layer-norm blocks with GELU instead
of post-LN with ReLU; AdamW with linear warmup and cosine decay; bf16 autocast;
and an exponential moving average of the weights, which is what we evaluate.
Our implementation is self-contained in \texttt{code/hw2/policies/act.py} (no
third-party library is vendored).

\textbf{Training configuration.} \trainSteps{} gradient steps, batch size
\batchSize, learning rate \learnRate{} (cosine), weight decay $10^{-4}$,
gradient-norm clip $1.0$, EMA decay $0.999$,
\resquantityactNOneZeroZeroSZeroPtZeroParams\,M parameters. Observations and
actions are standardised per dimension; crucially the statistics are fit on the
subset actually used by each condition, never on the full pool, so the 25-demo
condition cannot borrow information from the 100-demo pool. Seeds
$\{0,1,2\}$ control model init, data shuffling and the CVAE sampling.
Figure~\ref{fig:loss} shows the loss curves for all ACT runs.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/loss.png}
\caption{ACT training loss (left: total objective; right: action $L_1$) for
every condition and seed.}
\label{fig:loss}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Inference.} We use ACT's temporal ensembling (\execMode): the policy is
queried at every control step and all chunk predictions covering the current
timestep are averaged with exponential weights. Actions are de-standardised and
clipped to $[-1,1]$ before being passed to the simulator.

\section{Data ablation}
\textbf{Option A --- quantity.} Students were trained on nested subsets of the
same clean pool, so the 25-demo set is a strict subset of the 50-demo set,
which is a strict subset of the 100-demo set; nothing else changes.
Success rises from \resquantityactNTwoFiveSZeroPtZeroMean\% (25 demos) to
\resquantityactNFiveZeroSZeroPtZeroMean\% (50) to
\resquantityactNOneZeroZeroSZeroPtZeroMean\% (100); see
Figure~\ref{fig:quantity}. The paired comparison $25\!\rightarrow\!100$ gives
$\Delta=\cmpQtyTwentyFiveToHundredDelta$\%
[\cmpQtyTwentyFiveToHundredLo, \cmpQtyTwentyFiveToHundredHi],
McNemar $p=\cmpQtyTwentyFiveToHundredP$.

\textbf{Option B --- teacher action noise.} Further pools were collected with
Gaussian noise added to the teacher's action \emph{during} environment
interaction, $a = \mathrm{clip}(\pi(s)+\varepsilon,-1,1)$ with
$\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$, applied before the environment's own
\texttt{action\_scale} and actuator clipping. The stored action is the one
actually executed. Dataset size is held at 100 episodes and the pools are
\emph{not} filtered for success, so the independent variable is data quality.

A first attempt at $\sigma\in\{0.1,0.3\}$ produced \emph{no} usable contrast:
the teacher still succeeded \teacherSuccSigmaZeroPtOne\% and
\teacherSuccSigmaZeroPtThree\% of the time, because the noise is injected into a
\emph{closed-loop} delta-action policy, which simply corrects for it at the next
step. A quality ablation says nothing in a regime where quality has not actually
degraded, so we extended the sweep to $\sigma\in\{0.5,1,2\}$, where teacher
success falls to \teacherSuccSigmaZeroPtFive\%, \teacherSuccSigmaOnePtZero\% and
\teacherSuccSigmaTwoPtZero\%.

\textbf{The result is non-monotonic, and its sign depends on the inference
mode.} Under the pre-specified headline mode (temporal ensembling), students
trained on the noisy pools score \resnoiseactNOneZeroZeroSZeroPtFiveMean\%,
\resnoiseactNOneZeroZeroSOnePtZeroMean\% and
\resnoiseactNOneZeroZeroSTwoPtZeroMean\% against the clean baseline's
\resquantityactNOneZeroZeroSZeroPtZeroMean\%, i.e.\ moderate noise is roughly
neutral and heavy noise is catastrophic. Under re-planning every control step,
the same $\sigma=0.5$ checkpoints reach
\conenoiseactNOneZeroZeroSZeroPtFiveMean\% against the clean baseline's
\conequantityactNOneZeroZeroSZeroPtZeroMean\% --- that is, demonstrations from a
\emph{worse} teacher produce a substantially \emph{better} student
($\Delta=\cmpCOneNoiseLowDelta$\%
[\cmpCOneNoiseLowLo, \cmpCOneNoiseLowHi], $p=\cmpCOneNoiseLowP$), and the
gripper reaches the box in \conenoiseactNOneZeroZeroSZeroPtFiveBox\% of episodes
versus \conequantityactNOneZeroZeroSZeroPtZeroBox\%. Both comparisons appear in
Table~\ref{tab:paired}.

The mechanism is the one identified in Section~\ref{sec:fail}: the student's
binding constraint is not the optimality of the demonstrated actions but the
\emph{breadth of the state distribution} they cover, and perturbing the teacher
during interaction is precisely what broadens it. At $\sigma=0.5$ the coverage
gain outweighs the loss of demonstrator quality; by $\sigma=1$ the teacher
succeeds only \teacherSuccSigmaOnePtZero\% of the time and the demonstrations no
longer contain the task, so the student collapses. We note the confound
explicitly: that condition changes demonstrator quality and state coverage at
the same time, and the clean way to separate them --- inject noise for coverage
but keep only the episodes that still succeed --- is the experiment we would run
next. We also flag the obvious caveat that conclusions about demonstration
quality here are mode-dependent, which is itself a reason to report both modes
rather than one.

\section{Failure analysis}
\label{sec:fail}
We localised the teacher--student gap with four checks, reported here in the
order that rules out the cheapest explanations first.

\textbf{1. The demonstrations are faithfully recorded.} Replaying each stored
action sequence from the initial condition it was collected from reproduces the
stored observations: the reset state matches to \diagResetDev{} and the
deviation after one step is \diagReplayStepOneDev. The deviation then grows over
the episode, but that is chaotic amplification of floating-point noise in a
contact-rich simulation (a single-environment replay and a 512-environment
batched rollout do not execute bit-identical kernels), not a misalignment: a
misaligned pairing would be wrong at the first step. So the
(observation, action) pairing and the seeding are correct.

\textbf{2. The model fits the data.} Final training action $L_1$ is
\diagMaeTrainStd{} action standard deviations, and the one-step action error on
training states is \diagMaeTrainRaw{} in raw action units. This is not
underfitting.

\textbf{3. The failure is covariate shift, not generalisation of the action
map.} Evaluated at the states the \emph{teacher} visits, the student's action
error is \diagMaeTeacherRaw{} --- essentially the same as on its own training
states, so the learned action map generalises fine across initial conditions.
Evaluated at the states the \emph{student itself} visits, the error is
\diagMaeStudentRaw, a factor of \diagMaeRatio{} worse. The student is accurate
everywhere the teacher goes and badly wrong as soon as it leaves that
distribution. Consistently, evaluating on the initial conditions the
demonstrations were collected from gives only \diagTrainInitSucc\% (versus
\diagTestInitSucc\% on held-out ones): the student cannot reliably reproduce
trajectories whose own starting states it was trained on, so the problem is
closed-loop execution rather than memorisation or initial-condition coverage.

\textbf{4. Where the episodes actually die.} Figure~\ref{fig:failure} splits the
per-episode diagnostics by outcome, classified by a mutually exclusive taxonomy
computed from the per-episode diagnostics. Of \failNFail{} failures out of
\failNEval{} evaluation episodes, the modal class is
\textbf{``\failTopLabel''} with \failTopCount{} episodes (\failTopShare\% of all
failures); the remainder are \failOtherList. In other words the dominant
failure is \emph{not} a grasp or transport failure but a \textbf{reaching}
failure: in most failed episodes the gripper never comes within
$12\,\mathrm{mm}$ of the box at all (the gripper reaches the box in only
\failReachedBox\% of episodes), so the policy never gets far enough to attempt
a lift. That is notable because the box position \emph{relative to the gripper}
is given directly in the observation, so this is a servoing behaviour the
student fits accurately on-distribution and still fails to execute --- the
signature of drift rather than of a missing input.

Success is also threshold-sensitive, which is why we report the sweep: it rises
from \failSuccRadiusZeroPtZeroThree\% at a $3\,$cm tolerance to
\failSuccRadiusZeroPtZeroFive\% at our \successRadiusCm\,cm criterion and
saturates at \failSuccRadiusZeroPtOne\% by $10\,$cm, so a modest number of
episodes end just outside the tolerance while most end far from it.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/failure.png}
\caption{Failure analysis for the baseline student: distance traces split by
outcome, distribution of closest approach to the target, and the failure
taxonomy.}
\label{fig:failure}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Why this is the expected failure, and what it implies for the
ablations.} This is the compounding-error argument for behaviour cloning in its
textbook form: the student is trained only on the teacher's state distribution,
and the grasp is precisely the point where a small action error moves the system
into a state the teacher never demonstrates (box nudged, fingers misaligned),
from which there is no corrective behaviour in the data to imitate. Action
chunking bounds how fast error compounds \emph{within} a chunk, which is why
re-planning every step beats executing chunks open loop, but it cannot supply a
recovery behaviour that the demonstrations do not contain.

It also explains the shape of both ablations. More of the same on-distribution
demonstrations attacks coverage of \emph{initial} conditions, which
diagnostic~3 shows is not the binding constraint --- consistent with the modest
quantity effect we measure. And it explains the most interesting result in the
quality ablation: at $\sigma=0.5$ the demonstrations come from a
\teacherSuccSigmaZeroPtFive\% teacher rather than a
\teacherSuccSigmaZeroPtZero\% one, yet the student is not correspondingly worse
and reaches the box \emph{more} often, because perturbing the teacher during
interaction is exactly what broadens the state distribution the student is
trained on. Degrading the demonstrator and broadening coverage are confounded in
that condition, and the two effects push in opposite directions; separating them
(noise injected for coverage while keeping only successful episodes) is the
experiment we would run next.

\section{Reproducibility and reflection}
\textbf{Software.} Python \verpython, jax \verjax{} / jaxlib \verjaxlib{}
(CUDA\,12), flax \verflax, brax \verbrax, mujoco \vermujoco, mujoco-mjx
\vermujocomjx, \texttt{playground} \verplayground{} pinned at upstream commit
\texttt{\playgroundSha}, torch \vertorch, numpy \vernumpy. Hardware:
\gpuName{} on \hostName.

\textbf{Two environment-specific issues worth recording.} (i) The cluster's GPU
nodes ship no NVIDIA graphics userspace, so the EGL rendering path in the
assignment's reference snippet cannot work; all rendering here goes through a
software OSMesa stack configured from inside Python before \texttt{mujoco} is
imported, because PyOpenGL resolves \texttt{libOSMesa} through
\texttt{ctypes.util.find\_library}, which does not consult
\texttt{LD\_LIBRARY\_PATH}. (ii) \texttt{brax} 0.14.2 still calls
\texttt{jax.device\_put\_replicated}, which recent jax removed, so jax is
pinned to \verjax{} and flax to \verflax; installing the newest of each gives
an \texttt{AttributeError} only once PPO actually starts.

\textbf{Determinism.} MJX reproduces a given initial condition exactly
(single-environment and batched resets agree to \diagResetDev), but a replay of
the same action sequence diverges from the original rollout at a rate set by
floating-point noise, because batched and unbatched rollouts do not execute
identical kernels. Success labels are unaffected, but exact trajectory-level
reproduction of a stored episode should not be expected.

\textbf{Commands.} See \texttt{README.md}; the three stages are
\texttt{stage1\_teacher\_collect.py} (teacher + datasets + expert videos),
\texttt{stage2\_run\_conditions.py} (train + evaluate every condition), and
\texttt{stage3\_report.py} (aggregate, figures, tables, this PDF). Seeds are
\{0,1,2\} for students, \texttt{PRNGKey(7777)} split for the shared evaluation
initial conditions, and \texttt{PRNGKey}$(1000+\lfloor 1000\sigma \rfloor)$ for
demonstration collection.

\textbf{What more compute or time would buy.} The clearest limitation is the
teacher: our success criterion is strict and the PPO teacher is itself far from
perfect, which compresses the dynamic range of every student comparison. With
more time we would (i) train the teacher to a genuinely high success rate, or
use a scripted expert, so that student scores are not bounded by a mediocre
demonstrator, (ii) add a DAgger-style or noise-injected-recovery arm to attack
the grasp-failure mode directly rather than only measuring it, and (iii) run
more seeds, since even with paired evaluation the seed-to-seed spread
(visible as dots in Figure~\ref{fig:quantity}) is comparable to some of the
effects we are trying to resolve.
